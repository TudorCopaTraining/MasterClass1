Interactive Tool: EDI in AI for Sales and Recruitment

What is EDI in AI?

Artificial intelligence can support faster sales and recruitment decisions, but it can also reproduce existing inequalities when training data, criteria or processes are poorly designed. Applying Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) means ensuring that AI-assisted decisions provide fair access, respect individual differences and do not disadvantage people because of protected characteristics or personal circumstances.

This activity explores how AI choices can either remove or create barriers for customers, candidates and employees. Review each workplace challenge and choose the response that best supports inclusive practice.

The Scenario: You are advising Russell Taylor Group on the introduction of AI tools across sales and recruitment. The technology will support lead prioritisation, candidate screening, customer communication and interview processes. Your decisions must balance commercial efficiency with fair, accessible and human-centred practice.

Pillar 1: Disability and Neurodiversity in Recruitment

Challenge: An AI video-interview platform analyses spoken answers, eye contact and response speed. How should the recruitment team support candidates with disabilities, anxiety or neurodivergent communication styles?

❌ Created a Barrier: A rigid video-only process may disadvantage autistic candidates, people with speech or hearing impairments, candidates with anxiety and those who use assistive technology. Standardisation is not the same as fairness.
✅ Inclusive Design: Providing accessible alternatives and reasonable adjustments allows candidates to demonstrate job-related competence without being penalised for disability or communication style.

Pillar 2: Bridging Digital Exclusion

Challenge: The business wants candidates and prospective customers to interact through an AI chatbot before speaking to a member of staff. How should people with limited digital access or confidence be supported?

❌ Created a Barrier: A digital-only service can exclude people without reliable internet, suitable devices, digital confidence or accessible technology. It may reduce the diversity of the candidate and customer pool.
✅ Inclusive Design: A hybrid service uses AI for routine support while preserving meaningful access to people. This improves efficiency without making digital confidence a condition of participation.

Pillar 3: Language and Communication Inclusion

Challenge: AI is being introduced to write sales messages and screen CVs. How should the organisation treat candidates and customers who use English as an additional language or communicate differently?

✅ Inclusive Design: Focusing on job-related evidence and reviewing AI-generated communication for context prevents language differences from being mistaken for a lack of capability or customer value.
❌ Created a Barrier: Grammar-based scoring can act as a proxy for nationality, education or socioeconomic background. Unreviewed translation can also create confusing or culturally inappropriate sales communication.

Pillar 4: Managing Socioeconomic Impacts

Challenge: AI tools use location, employment history and previous purchasing behaviour to rank sales leads and recruitment candidates. How should the organisation prevent socioeconomic disadvantage?

❌ Created a Barrier: Postcode and employment gaps can act as proxies for income, disability, caring responsibilities or local deprivation. Using them without safeguards can systematically deprioritise capable candidates and legitimate customers.
✅ Inclusive Design: Restricting proxy variables, auditing outcomes and keeping humans accountable helps ensure that AI supports relevant business decisions without reinforcing historic disadvantage.
